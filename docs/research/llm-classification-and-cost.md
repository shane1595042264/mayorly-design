## Bottom line

**Architecture.** Build it as two separate systems on two different cadences, exactly mirroring the TnT-LLM split ([arXiv:2403.12173](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12173)):

1. **Chest induction** (rare, batch, expensive model): looks at accumulated inbox items and *proposes* chests. Runs weekly, or when the "unfiled" pile crosses a threshold. Never runs per item.
2. **Chest assignment** (per item, cheap model, batched): files an item into an existing chest, or into `__UNFILED__`. Cannot create chests.

Letting the online classifier create chests is the single mechanism that produces 40 near-duplicate chests. Separate the two and sprawl becomes a design parameter instead of an emergent disaster.

**Anti-sprawl, ranked by evidence strength:**
- Give every chest a written **inclusion criterion**, not just a name. This is LLooM's core move ("concepts, defined by explicit inclusion criteria" — [arXiv:2404.12259](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12259)). Two chests with names but no criteria are indistinguishable to the classifier; two chests with criteria are separable.
- **Minimum population before a chest exists.** Straight from Clio ("a minimum threshold for the number of unique users or conversations, so that low-frequency topics... aren't inadvertently exposed" — [anthropic.com/research/clio](https://www.anthropic.com/research/clio)). Require ≥4 items before a chest is born; until then they sit in `__UNFILED__`. This also happens to be great game design: chests appearing on the farm should feel earned.
- **Periodic whole-taxonomy refactor with merge/split/rename/reorder**, driven by the classifier's own confusion, not by human aesthetics. TaxMorph ([arXiv:2601.18375](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18375)) shows LLM-refactored taxonomies beat human-curated ones by up to **+2.9pp F1**, and that the win comes precisely because they "align more closely with the model's actual confusion patterns during classification."
- **Go hierarchical past ~20 chests.** Anthropic's own classification guide says this explicitly ([ticket-routing guide](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/use-case-guides/ticket-routing)): "Use a taxonomic hierarchy for cases with 20+ intent categories."

**When to ask.** Ask only when a *named, consequential* field is missing — recurrence, deadline, or which of exactly two chests — never for general vagueness. Implement it as **two fields the classifier already returns** (`action_confidence` and `missing_field`), following the uncertainty-decomposition pattern that was explicitly designed for your constraints (black-box API, latency budget, no labeled trajectories) — [arXiv:2606.19559](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19559). Use **verbalized confidence**, not logprobs: the Anthropic API doesn't expose logprobs anyway, and "Just Ask for Calibration" ([arXiv:2305.14975](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14975)) found verbalized confidence from RLHF'd models is *better* calibrated than the model's own conditional probabilities, "often reducing the expected calibration error by a relative 50%." Then **queue questions to the morning conversation** and cap them (≤2/day). Rationale: clarification is a sequential policy problem where interaction cost is a first-class term (RegretBench, [arXiv:2607.21143](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21143)), and a good policy asks *rarely* — the information-gain-trained clarifier in [arXiv:2606.03135](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03135) improved success 3.7% while "adding only 0.3 total interaction steps on average."

**Model + cost.** `claude-haiku-4-5-20251001` for per-item filing (batched, terse output), `claude-sonnet-5` for the morning planner and the weekly chest refactor.

> **≈ $0.045 per active user per day ≈ $1.35/user/month** at 20 classifications + 1 morning plan + 2 clarifications + evening wrap.
> An all-Haiku variant lands at **≈ $0.031/day ≈ $0.93/month**.
> Naively defaulting to `claude-opus-5` with stock settings lands at **≈ $0.36/day ≈ $10.80/month** — a ~8x blowout that would eat a $5/mo subscription whole.

Full arithmetic and the four traps that cause the blowout are in §3.

**Privacy, the one non-obvious finding.** Do **not** put user-invented chest names into the JSON Schema `enum`. Anthropic's data-retention doc states that with structured outputs or `strict: true` tools, "the API compiles JSON schemas into grammars that are **cached separately from message content**. These cached schemas **do not receive the same PHI protections** as prompts and responses" and are retained "for up to 24 hours since last use" ([API and data retention](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/api-and-data-retention)). Chest names in a todo app are user-authored and can be identifying ("Dr. Nguyen knee follow-ups"). Put the taxonomy in *message content*; keep the schema a fixed shape with an integer index. This is also the cheaper choice — see §3.3.

## 1. Emergent classification into a growing chest set

### 1.1 The core pattern: two phases, two cadences

TnT-LLM (Microsoft, applied to Bing Copilot conversations — [arXiv:2403.12173](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12173)) is the closest published match to todoFarm's problem: an under-specified label space, no annotations, open-ended free text. Verbatim from the abstract:

> "We propose TnT-LLM, a two-phase framework... In the first phase, we introduce a zero-shot, multi-stage reasoning approach which enables LLMs to produce and refine a label taxonomy iteratively. In the second phase, LLMs are used as data labelers that yield training samples so that lightweight supervised classifiers can be reliably built, deployed, and served at scale."

Two things to take and one to leave:

- **Take: the phase split.** Taxonomy induction is iterative and refinement-based; assignment is a separate, cheap, high-volume operation. In todoFarm terms, chest creation is a *ritual* (an NPC event, weekly-ish) and chest filing is *plumbing*.
- **Take: iterative refinement.** The taxonomy is not generated once from the first 10 items. It is proposed, then repeatedly revised against more data.
- **Leave: the distilled supervised classifier.** TnT-LLM's phase 2 trains a lightweight model on LLM pseudo-labels because it runs at Bing scale. You run at 20 items/user/day. Just call the LLM.

**Honest gap:** I could not extract TnT-LLM's Phase 1 method section. A 2026 independent evaluation ([arXiv:2608.01592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01592), Nakashima et al., applying TnT-LLM to software-engineering artifacts) found "TnT-LLM constructs high-quality taxonomies comparable to human-defined ones" but at meaningfully higher compute cost than a cheaper alternative it benchmarks against (CLIMB, reported as "15--40× faster and 8--49× cheaper"). **Gap:** I could not locate the CLIMB paper itself through arXiv title search — treat that comparison as second-hand.

### 1.2 Keeping the taxonomy from sprawling

Four mechanisms, strongest evidence first.

**(a) Inclusion criteria, not names.** LLooM's whole thesis ([arXiv:2404.12259](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12259), Lam et al., Stanford/UW) is that LLM-induced concepts beat topic models because they are "high-level concepts, **defined by explicit inclusion criteria**." Their worked example: BERTopic outputs `"women, power, female"`; LLooM outputs `"Criticism of traditional gender roles"`. The second is classifiable; the first is not. LLooM "iteratively synthesize[s] sampled text and propose[s] human-interpretable concepts of increasing generality," and beat topic models "in terms of quality and data coverage."

For todoFarm: every chest row in SQLite carries `name`, `criterion` (one sentence, what belongs), `anti_criterion` (one sentence, what does *not* belong and where it goes instead). The anti-criterion is the near-duplicate killer — it's the field that says "Errands ≠ Admin: Errands require leaving the house."

**(b) Minimum population before birth.** Clio ([arXiv:2412.13678](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13678); [anthropic.com/research/clio](https://www.anthropic.com/research/clio)) builds its hierarchy by extracting facets → clustering → LLM-naming each cluster → building a multi-level hierarchy, with a hard floor: "We also have a minimum threshold for the number of unique users or conversations, so that low-frequency topics... aren't inadvertently exposed." Clio's floor is a *privacy* control; yours is a *sprawl* control, but the mechanism is identical. A chest with one item in it is not a category, it's a note.

**(c) Refactor against the model's confusion, on a schedule.** TaxMorph ([arXiv:2601.18375](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18375), Golde/Jedema/Krishnan/Le) is the most directly useful 2026 result here. It "uses large language models to transform entire taxonomies through operations such as **renaming, merging, splitting, and reordering**," and — critically — "Unlike prior work, our method revises the **full hierarchy**" rather than patching one node. Results: "+2.9pp. in F1" over human-curated taxonomies.

Their diagnosis of *why* is the part to internalize:

> "human-curated taxonomies lead to more easily separable clusters in embedding space. However, the LLM-refined taxonomies align more closely with the model's actual confusion patterns during classification. In other words, even though they are harder to separate, they better reflect the model's inductive biases."

Concrete build: log every low-confidence classification and every user-initiated re-file as a `(item, predicted_chest, actual_chest)` triple. The weekly refactor call gets the chest roster **plus this confusion log** and is asked to emit merge/split/rename operations. That is the loop TaxMorph validates. It also gives you a beautiful game mechanic — the NPC proposing "I think these two chests are the same thing, should I combine them?" is exactly the interaction the paper's method describes.

**(d) Hierarchy past 20.** Anthropic's [ticket-routing guide](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/use-case-guides/ticket-routing) has a section literally titled "Use a taxonomic hierarchy for cases with 20+ intent categories":

> "As the number of classes grows, the number of examples required also expands, potentially making the prompt unwieldy. As an alternative, you can consider implementing a hierarchical classification system using a mixture of classifiers."

With the tradeoff named: "**Cons - increased latency:** Be advised that multiple classifiers can lead to increased latency, and Anthropic recommends implementing this approach with the fastest model, Haiku."

For todoFarm this maps naturally onto farm geography: regions (barn / house / field) as the top level, chests within them as leaves. You get a UX affordance and a classification-accuracy mechanism from the same structure.

### 1.3 Open-set: the escape hatch is not optional

**This part is reasoning from the API's documented mechanics, not from a cited paper — I want to be clear about that.** Structured outputs use "grammar-constrained sampling" ([structured outputs doc](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/structured-outputs)). If you constrain the output to a closed enum of existing chests, the model *physically cannot* say "none of these." It will pick the least-wrong one, silently, with high stated confidence, forever. Every item lands somewhere; nothing looks broken; the taxonomy quietly rots.

So the assignment schema always includes:
- `__UNFILED__` — "this doesn't belong in any existing chest." These accumulate and are the input to the next induction run.
- `__AMBIGUOUS__` — "two chests are equally right." These are the input to the next merge decision.

Anthropic's guide notes the failure mode this prevents in the traditional-ML framing: "Traditional ML systems often struggle with outliers and ambiguous inputs, frequently misclassifying them or defaulting to a catch-all category."

### 1.4 Re-classification when the taxonomy moves

Design (not directly cited — see the gap note):

- `chests` rows are versioned; `items` carry `chest_id` + `taxonomy_version` + `assignment_reason`.
- A **merge** is free: re-point rows, no LLM call. A **rename** is free. A **split** is the only expensive op — it requires re-filing the affected chest's contents, which is one batched call over ~20-40 items.
- Never re-file the whole corpus. Only touch chests the refactor operation actually named.
- Show the user the diff. A game NPC reorganizing your stuff without telling you is a horror story; an NPC saying "I moved 6 things from Errands to Health, want to see?" is a feature.

**Honest gap:** I found no paper specifically on *incremental re-labeling cost under taxonomy drift* at personal-todo scale. TaxMorph refactors the taxonomy and re-evaluates, but its benchmarks are static corpora, not a live stream with a user watching. The design above is engineering judgment, not a cited result.

## 2. Knowing when to ask

### 2.1 What the literature says the default failure is

Two 2025-2026 benchmarks disagree with each other in a way that matters for you.

**ClarifyMT-Bench** ([arXiv:2512.21120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.21120), 6,120 multi-turn dialogues, 10 models) finds **under-**asking is the norm:

> "Evaluating ten representative LLMs uncovers a consistent **under-clarification bias**: LLMs tend to answer prematurely, and performance degrades as dialogue depth increases."

**RegretBench** ([arXiv:2607.21143](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21143)) reframes it so both failures are visible at once:

> "Ambiguous user requests make clarification a sequential decision problem for conversational LLM assistants: they must decide **whether to ask, what to ask, when to stop, and when to answer**... final success alone is insufficient, as models with similar accuracy can differ substantially in efficiency, robustness to user behaviors, and stopping decisions."

Reconciling them for todoFarm: the *model's* bias is to under-ask, but your *product* has a chatty companion NPC whose entire personality pushes toward asking. Those cancel out badly — you will over-ask. So build the budget in from day one, and measure interaction cost as a first-class metric (RegretBench's regret objective; ClarifyCodeBench's "Turn-discounted Key Question Rate," [arXiv:2607.00711](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00711), which explicitly penalizes inefficient questioning).

### 2.2 The decision rule that's actually right

**Ask iff the answer would change the outcome.** The cleanest statement of this is Zhang, Knox & Choi, ICLR 2025 ([arXiv:2410.13788](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13788)):

> "we assign preference labels by simulating their expected outcomes in **future turns**. This allows LLMs to learn to ask clarifying questions when it can generate responses that are tailored to each user interpretation in future turns."

And they measured the "when to ask" judgment separately: "our method can be used to train models to **judiciously determine when to ask** clarifying questions, directly answering the question when clarification is unnecessary... a 3% improvement in accuracy of such judgments."

Their diagnosis of the root cause is worth quoting because it applies directly to how you'd naively build this: "We speculate this is caused by current preference data labeling practice, where LLM responses are evaluated only on their **prior contexts**." A classifier that only sees the one-liner will either always ask or never ask. It has to be reasoning about the *downstream consequence* of getting it wrong.

Operationalized for todoFarm — the question is worth asking only when the answer flips a **behavior**:
| Missing field | Flips behavior? | Ask? |
|---|---|---|
| Recurrence ("brush my teeth" — daily or once?) | Yes: daily vs one-shot is a totally different object | **Ask** |
| Hard deadline ("renew passport") | Yes: changes morning-plan ordering | **Ask** |
| Which of 2 chests, both plausible | No: user can drag it | **File, don't ask** |
| Duration estimate | No: planner can guess and adjust | **Guess** |
| Vague wording ("deal with the thing") | Yes, but a question won't help — user won't remember either | **File to `__UNFILED__`, surface visually** |

That last row matters. Underspecified ≠ askable.

### 2.3 How to detect it, given a black-box API

The most useful paper here is **Uncertainty Decomposition for Clarification Seeking in LLM Agents** ([arXiv:2606.19559](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19559)), because its framing *is* your deployment situation, verbatim:

> "Practical deployment constraints -- black-box APIs, interactive latency budgets, and the absence of labeled trajectories -- rule out logprob-based, multi-sampling, and training-based methods, leaving **prompt-based estimation** as the most viable family for surfacing such signals at deployment time. We answer this call with a simple prompt-based decomposition that separates **action confidence** from **request uncertainty**."

Reported: "improves clarification F1 on ALFWorld-Clarification by **73% over ReAct+UE** and by 36% over UAM," across five backbones.

Two separate fields, not one. "I'm 90% sure this goes in Health" and "the item itself is missing its recurrence" are orthogonal, and collapsing them into a single confidence score destroys the signal.

**Calibrating the confidence number.** Two supporting results:
- "Just Ask for Calibration" ([arXiv:2305.14975](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14975)): "For RLHF-LMs such as ChatGPT, GPT-4, and Claude, we find that **verbalized confidences emitted as output tokens are typically better-calibrated than the model's conditional probabilities**... often reducing the expected calibration error by a relative 50%." Convenient, since the Anthropic API doesn't expose logprobs.
- Anthropic's own "Language Models (Mostly) Know What They Know" ([arXiv:2207.05221](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05221)): "larger models are well-calibrated on diverse multiple choice and true/false questions **when they are provided in the right format**." So make the confidence field a small **enum** (`certain` / `probable` / `guessing`), not a free float. This is also forced by the API — structured outputs don't support numeric `minimum`/`maximum` constraints (§3.3), so you couldn't bound a float anyway.

**Second-gate escalation, when Gate A is borderline.** Input Clarification Ensembling ([arXiv:2311.08718](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08718), Hou et al., MIT/UCSB, updated 2024): "generates a set of clarifications for the input, feeds them into an LLM, and ensembles the corresponding predictions... when aleatoric uncertainty arises from ambiguity or under-specification in LLM inputs, this approach makes it possible to factor an (unclarified) LLM's predictions into separate aleatoric and epistemic terms." Code at `https://github.com/UCSB-NLP-Chang/llm_uncertainty`.

Read the result: **disagreement across clarified variants = genuine ambiguity = ask. Agreement despite low confidence = model ignorance = don't ask, just file.** Exactly the discrimination you want.

⚠️ **API gotcha that changes how you implement this.** You cannot do temperature-based self-consistency sampling on the current models. Per the [Sonnet 5 migration guide](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/sonnet-5/migration-guide): "**Sampling parameters removed:** Sampling parameters (`temperature`, `top_p`, `top_k`) set to a non-default value are not accepted and return a 400 error." Same on Opus 5. Fortunately, input-clarification ensembling perturbs the *input*, not the sampling — so it still works. (If you specifically want temperature-based ensembling, the same guide notes "`temperature` and `top_p` work on Claude Haiku 4.5 (one at a time, not both)." That's a real reason to keep Haiku in the stack.)

**A technique you can't use:** Sparse Neurons Carry Strong Signals of Question Ambiguity ([arXiv:2509.13664](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.13664)) finds ambiguity is "linearly encoded in the internal representations of LLMs," detectable from "as few as one" neuron, with probes that "outperform prompting-based and representation-based baselines." Genuinely the best-performing detector in the literature — and completely unavailable through a hosted API. Noting it so you don't spend a week looking for it.

### 2.4 Not over-asking

Three levers, all evidenced:

1. **A hard budget.** The information-gain-optimized clarifier ([arXiv:2606.03135](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03135)) got "+3.7%" success while "adding only **0.3 total interaction steps on average**." A good policy barely asks. Cap at ≤2 questions/day, enforced in your code, not in the prompt.

2. **Batch questions into the morning ritual.** Don't interrupt the dump. The dump is a flow state — that's the product's whole promise. Queue `needs_clarification` items and let the NPC raise them during the morning planning conversation, where the user has already context-switched into planning mode. This is a pure interaction-cost reduction in RegretBench's sense, with no accuracy loss.

3. **Learn per-user so you stop asking the same thing.** This is the most on-brand finding for todoFarm's relationship mechanic. **CAPA** ([arXiv:2607.26611](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26611)) formalizes exactly this as "personalized ambiguity adaptation":

   > "coding requests often contain ambiguities that recur in **user-specific** ways across tasks and sessions... given a user's previously resolved coding sessions and a new ambiguous request, an assistant should identify the recurring ambiguity pattern, produce the intended executable solution, and **minimize clarification**."

   They evaluate on "turns-to-completion" and propose "same-user history gating as a lightweight inference-time method." For you: every resolved clarification writes a **rule** into SQLite ("hygiene items default to daily," "anything mentioning Dr. → Health chest, no question"). Those rules go into the classifier prefix. The NPC asking *fewer* questions as hearts go up is then not flavor text — it's a measured mechanism with a benchmark behind it.

### 2.5 Security note (matters later, not now)

**ASPI** ([arXiv:2605.17324](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17324), Scale AI; code at `https://github.com/scaleapi/aspi`) found that being in a clarification-seeking state dramatically amplifies prompt-injection vulnerability:

> "clarification-seeking consistently and substantially amplifies vulnerability. For instance, attack success rises from **1.8% to 34.0% for o3** and from 2.2% to 35.7% for Gemini-3-Flash."

Today todoFarm is safe: the only content source is the user typing at their own keyboard. **This becomes live the moment you add email/calendar ingestion to the inbox** — which is an obvious v2 feature ("email dr about knee" wants to become "here's the email"). At that point, the clarification channel is the vulnerable one, and the mitigation is to never let ingested third-party text influence the *question* the NPC asks, only the item being classified.

### 3.2 Structured output: use `output_config.format`, keep the schema frozen

Structured outputs are **GA, no beta header** ([doc](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/structured-outputs)), supported on `claude-opus-5`, `claude-sonnet-5`, and `claude-haiku-4-5-20251001`. Shape:

```json
{
  "model": "claude-haiku-4-5-20251001",
  "max_tokens": 1024,
  "messages": [...],
  "output_config": {
    "format": {
      "type": "json_schema",
      "schema": { "type": "object", "properties": {...},
                  "required": [...], "additionalProperties": false }
    }
  }
}
```

Note: `output_format` is deprecated; it moved to `output_config.format`. Prefill is gone on all three models, so this (or system-prompt instruction) is the only way to force JSON.

**Schema limitations that bite this design:**
- No numeric constraints (`minimum`, `maximum`, `multipleOf`) → **can't bound a confidence float**. Use an enum. (Which §2.3 says you should anyway.)
- No string constraints (`minLength`, `maxLength`) → can't cap the NPC's question length in the schema. Do it in the prompt.
- Array `minItems` only 0 or 1.
- `additionalProperties` must be `false`.
- No recursive schemas → a nested chest hierarchy can't be expressed recursively. Flatten to `region_id` + `chest_id`.

**Grammar caching is why the schema must be frozen:**
> "**Automatic caching:** Compiled grammars cached for 24 hours. **Cache invalidation:** Occurs when schema **structure** or the tool set changes (not when changing `name`/`description` only)... **First request latency:** Additional latency during initial grammar compilation."

A dynamic `enum` of chest IDs changes structure every time a chest is born → grammar recompile → latency spike on the exact request where the user just watched a new chest appear. Freeze the schema; put the roster in message content; return an index.

**Structured outputs vs strict tool use.** The doc's own table: `output_config.format` "controls Claude's response format"; `strict: true` "validates tool parameters." For a pure classifier that returns one object, structured outputs is the right tool and avoids the tool-use system-prompt overhead (286-496 tokens depending on model, per the pricing page's tool-use table). Reach for tools only if you want the NPC to emit conversational `text` *and* a structured decision in the same turn.

### 3.3 Prompt caching: batching beats caching for this workload

Mechanics ([doc](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-caching)): 5m TTL default (1.25× write), 1h TTL (2× write), reads at 0.1× base. Max 4 explicit breakpoints, 20-block lookback. Render order `tools` → `system` → `messages`.

Silent invalidators that will hit todoFarm specifically:
- **Tool definition changes** — full invalidation. Adding a chest must not change the tool list.
- **Effort setting changes** — full invalidation. Don't vary effort per item.
- **Thinking parameter changes** — full invalidation.

**The uncomfortable finding:** caching is a poor fit for todoFarm's *shape*. The 5-minute TTL assumes bursty traffic; your user dumps three items at 9am, one at noon, four at 6pm. Every scattered call is a cache **write** (1.25×), never a read. Worked numbers on Sonnet 5 with a 2,600-token prefix and five scattered calls:

- No caching: 5 × 2,600 × $2/M = **$0.026**
- 5m caching, all writes: 5 × 2,600 × $2.50/M = **$0.0325** (*worse*)
- 1h caching, all five inside one hour: 1 write ($0.0104) + 4 reads ($0.0021) = **$0.0125**

So: caching helps only if you *shape* the traffic. **Batching removes the prefix entirely instead of discounting it** — one call for N items pays the prefix once at full price, which beats every caching scenario above. Use batching as the primary lever and caching as the secondary one (for the multi-turn morning conversation, where turns genuinely land inside 5 minutes).

Also note Haiku 4.5's 4,096-token minimum cacheable prefix. A realistic classification prefix is ~2,000 tokens, so **on Haiku 4.5 caching silently does nothing.** No error, no warning, just full-price input forever. Verify with `usage.cache_read_input_tokens` — if it's always 0, that's why.

### 3.4 Streaming

From the [streaming doc](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/streaming): `message_start` → per-block `content_block_start` / `content_block_delta`* / `content_block_stop` → `message_delta`* → `message_stop`. Tool inputs arrive as `input_json_delta` with **partial JSON strings**, accumulated and parsed at `content_block_stop`.

The line that matters for your UI: *"Current models only support emitting one complete key and value property from `input` at a time. As such, when using tools, there may be delays between streaming events while the model is working."* So a structured classification result arrives essentially key-by-key with pauses, not smoothly. **Recommendation:** don't stream the classification at all (it's ~40 output tokens; latency is dominated by TTFT anyway). **Do** stream the morning planning conversation and the clarifying dialogue, where the NPC's chattiness is the product.

`eager_input_streaming: true` on a tool definition enables fine-grained streaming — and note it is **not** a beta feature, it's a field on the tool definition with the regular `.stream()` call.

One trap: with thinking on (default on Sonnet 5/Opus 5), *"a response can begin with one or more `thinking` blocks before the first `text` block"* and `display` defaults to `"omitted"`, so **your NPC's dialogue will appear to hang** while invisible thinking streams. Either set `thinking: {type: "disabled"}` on chat turns, or set `display: "summarized"` and render the reasoning as the NPC "thinking" (which, for a Stardew-style companion, is arguably a great animation cue).

## 4. Privacy

### 4.1 What actually has to cross the network

todoFarm holds medical one-liners ("email dr about knee"), financial ones, relationship ones. The design goal is that the proxy sees the *minimum semantic payload* and nothing else.

**Must cross:**
- The item text itself (irreducible — it's what's being classified)
- The chest roster: names + inclusion criteria (needed to classify against)
- A small learned-preferences block (the CAPA-style rules from §2.4)
- For the morning plan only: open task titles + time preferences

**Must not cross, and doesn't need to:**
- User identity (email, name, device ID) — use a random per-install token
- Completion history, streaks, relationship/heart level → collapse locally into `"relationship: warm, 4 months"`
- Timestamps beyond "it's Tuesday morning"
- Chest contents for chests not involved in this decision
- Anything at all during the evening put-back — that's `UPDATE tasks SET ...` in SQLite, not an LLM call

The Phaser/Tauri/SQLite-local-first architecture is already the right shape. Push more of the deterministic work down into SQLite: recurrence math, due-date rollover, streak counting, put-back. Every one of those you move local is a network call you don't make and a payload you don't send.

### 4.3 The schema-leakage finding

The single most actionable privacy item, and one I would not have guessed:

> "When using structured outputs or tools with `strict: true`, the API compiles JSON schemas into grammars that are **cached separately from message content. These cached schemas do not receive the same PHI protections as prompts and responses. Do not include PHI in JSON schema definitions.** This restriction applies to schema **property names, `enum` values, `const` values, and `pattern` regular expressions.**"
> — [API and data retention § PHI handling guidelines](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/api-and-data-retention)

That's written for HIPAA, but the *mechanism* is general: schemas live on a different, less-protected, 24-hour-retained path than message content. In todoFarm, the tempting design is a dynamic `enum` of live chest names — and chest names are user-authored free text that can name a doctor, a condition, an ex-partner, an employer.

**Do this instead:**
```json
// schema — frozen, generic, no user content, no PHI, grammar cache stable
{
  "type": "object",
  "properties": {
    "item_index":   {"type": "integer"},
    "chest_index":  {"type": "integer"},          // index into roster sent in the message
    "unfiled":      {"type": "boolean"},
    "action_confidence": {"enum": ["certain","probable","guessing"]},
    "missing_field":     {"enum": ["none","recurrence","deadline","chest_tie"]}
  },
  "required": ["item_index","chest_index","unfiled","action_confidence","missing_field"],
  "additionalProperties": false
}
```
The chest roster (names + criteria) goes in the **user message**, where it's covered by standard retention/ZDR. The model returns an index; your app maps it. Three wins from one decision: better privacy posture, stable grammar cache (no recompile latency when a chest is born), and fewer output tokens.

### 4.4 The Railway proxy

Your proxy is a bigger privacy risk than Anthropic, because it's the piece you control and the piece nobody audits.

- **Never log request or response bodies.** Log status, latency, token counts, anonymous install token. Nothing else. Set this on day one — retrofitting log redaction after a support incident never happens.
- **No user identifiers in the payload.** Random per-install UUID, rotatable from the app, not derived from email or hardware ID.
- The proxy is architecturally required anyway: *"CORS is not supported for organizations with ZDR arrangements. To make API calls from browser-based applications, **route requests through a backend proxy server**."* ([API and data retention](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/api-and-data-retention)). Your design is already ZDR-compatible.
- **Get ZDR before you have users.** It's per-organization, enabled by the account team, and "enablement does not automatically extend to other organizations under the same account." For a commercially distributed app holding people's medical todos, this is table stakes and it's free to ask for.
- **Rate-limit per install token** — otherwise your API key is a free LLM for anyone who cracks the binary.
- **Skip the Batch API** for user content (29-day retention, not ZDR-eligible), even for the weekly refactor.
- **Ship a "private chest"** whose contents never leave the device. Items in it are filed by hand and excluded from every prompt including the morning plan. It costs you one boolean and one `WHERE` clause, and it's the feature that makes the app trustworthy enough for the medical and financial one-liners that are half its value.
- **Be able to say all of this in one screen.** "Your tasks live in a file on your Mac. When the farmhand sorts something, that one line goes to Anthropic and is deleted; it is never used to train anything. Private chests never leave your Mac." That paragraph is a commercial asset, and every recommendation above exists to make it true.

Truth: TaxMorph does not feed a confusion log into refactoring. **2. A moody NPC companion with Stardew-style heart levels is exactly a consumer-facing chatbot, and the design's whole appeal is that it does not feel like an API. **3. The "≥4 items before a chest is born" rule contradicts the product premise.** todoFarm "starts nearly empty", chests are the primary UI object, and the NPC "can always invent new ones". Clio's threshold is a privacy control over millions of conversations, not a taxonomy-quality control for one person's 20 items a day; the report says the mechanism is "identical" without noting the population difference. Cold start is never addressed at all, and the cost model quietly assumes a 15-chest roster already exists.

**4. So the grammar that previously made an invalid chest physically unsayable now permits any integer. The claimed "three wins from one decision" is really three wins and one new failure mode needing an app-side bounds check and a re-ask path. Note this is the mirror image of the report's own §1.3 argument.

**5. Input Clarification Ensembling is recommended without being costed.** §2.3 proposes it as a second gate; it requires generating N clarified variants and running N inferences per borderline item. That is a multiple of the per-item budget §3 spends the whole section minimizing, and it sits inside the interactive path.

**6. The report's own source warns that TnT-LLM over-generates taxonomy complexity.** From the same abstract it cites for the CLIMB numbers: "TnT-LLM constructs high-quality taxonomies comparable to human-defined ones but incurs substantially higher cost and runtime and **tends to generate overly complex taxonomies**." That is precisely the sprawl failure the report is trying to engineer against, found in the method it adopts. Quoted selectively. [arXiv:2608.01592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01592)

**7. ZDR is presented as obtainable without a fallback.** It is per-organization, enabled by an account team through sales, and "enablement does not automatically extend to other organizations under the same account". A solo developer shipping a $5/month todo app is unlikely to get it. The far more probable world is standard 30-day retention, and the report has no plan for it, including no revised version of the one-screen privacy paragraph it calls "a commercial asset".

**8. Railway proxy cost, latency, and cold starts are excluded** from a per-user economics section otherwise precise to the tenth of a cent. So is any per-install spend ceiling, which is the actual defense against a cracked binary; "rate-limit per install token" is named but never sized.

**9. The Batch API rejection leans on a ZDR arrangement you probably will not have,** and it discards the one workload batching genuinely fits: the weekly, non-interactive chest refactor.

## Net assessment

**The verifiable factual layer is unusually clean, and I want to say that plainly rather than manufacture errors.** I fetched every primary source that carries weight and found no fabrications:

- The full pricing table reproduces **exactly**, including the Fable 5.1 0.025x cache-hit footnote and the note that Sonnet 5's $2/$10 introductory price is now standard and the September 1 increase "will not occur".
- **All 21 arXiv IDs resolve to real papers** with the titles and first authors claimed, including the eleven 2026-dated ones that are the natural place for a fabricated citation to hide.
- Every load-bearing quotation I could check came back **verbatim**: the TaxMorph abstract, the Uncertainty Decomposition deployment-constraints passage and its 73%/36% figures, the 3.7%/0.3-steps result, ASPI's 1.8%→34.0% and 2.2%→35.7%, CLIMB's 15–40×/8–49×, Clio's minimum-threshold sentence, and all four ticket-routing quotes.

So is every row of the §4.2 table: ZDR's definition, per-organization enablement, the CORS/proxy requirement, the 2-year flagged-content retention, Batch at "29-day retention; async storage required", structured outputs at "Yes (qualified)" with 24-hour schema caching, and prompt caching as eligible.

**Trust the numbers, the citations, and the API facts. Distrust the leaps.** All eight corrections are synthesis errors, one flat factual slip (prefill on Haiku), one arithmetic outlier, and six overclaims or internal inconsistencies, not invented sources. The report is also honest about its own gaps, and its self-declared unknowns were accurate.

The omissions are where the real risk sits, and two of them are ship-blockers rather than nits: **Haiku 4.5's October 2026 retirement date**, which undermines the model choice threading through every recommendation and every cost figure, and the **AUP's AI-disclosure requirement**, which is a compliance obligation for the exact product being built and appears nowhere. Add the cold-start contradiction, and the practical verdict is that the research is sound but the design synthesis needs one more pass before anyone builds to it.
