# Prior art: does this game already exist?

## Verdict

**No — the exact four-pillar concept does not exist today, and I am confident in that.** Across roughly a dozen independent research angles (App Store, Mac App Store, Steam, itch.io, GitHub, Product Hunt, Hacker News, Nexus Mods), not one shipping product combines 16-bit cozy farm/life-sim aesthetics + a resident NPC + that NPC being LLM-backed and triaging a messy personal task inbox with clarifying questions + a genuinely usable task manager. The market splits into three clusters that never touch: (a) cozy/pixel productivity apps with **zero AI** — several of which advertise "No generative AI was used" as a *feature* ([Quest Board](https://weidengeist.itch.io/questboard), [Komodore's TO-DO APP](https://komodore.itch.io/komodore-todo), [Typing Farmer](https://hxdbk.itch.io/typing-farmer), [Productivity Desktop Pet](https://glitchedinorbit.itch.io/desktop-pet)); (b) LLM-backed desktop characters with **no task management at all** ([Dinoki](https://dinoki.ai/), [Sato](https://www.sato.host/), [CATAI](https://github.com/wil-pe/CATAI), [Buddy](https://buddy.artiphik.com/), [Masko](https://masko.ai/)); (c) LLM task triage with **no character and no aesthetic** ([Todoist Assist](https://www.todoist.com/todoist-assist), [Taskade](https://www.taskade.com/ai/tasks), [goblin.tools](https://goblin.tools/)). The single artifact touching all four pillars at once is [DeskRPG](https://github.com/dandacompany/deskrpg), a self-hosted multiplayer *office* for teams. Two caveats on confidence: Reddit was unreachable in this sweep, so community demand quotes are unverified; and a handful of candidates rest on store-listing text rather than hands-on use.

## What each one gets right, and where it stops

**DeskRPG** is the only artifact where all four pillars co-exist. Its `src/lib/task-prompt.js` injects a "Task Management Protocol" that makes each in-world NPC classify a chat message as work-instruction vs. small talk, write a sub-50-char title plus a 1–2 sentence summary, and file it on a board ([repo](https://github.com/dandacompany/deskrpg)). That is genuine in-character LLM capture. But the genre is a corporate office built on LPC office tilesets with zero crop/season/energy loop; it is a multiplayer browser app at `localhost:3000`, not a Mac app; and the relationship is inverted — NPCs are agents you delegate work *to*, who then execute it and walk over to deliver reports. Vagueness handling is absent: the only mandated question is a yes/no "Would you like me to register this as a task?"

**Spirit City: Lofi Sessions** is the commercial proof that the shell works. It ships a to-do list, habit tracker, journal, session timer, and productivity tracker inside a cozy sim, native on Apple Silicon, and sits at 16,276 positive out of 16,676 reviews ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2113850/Spirit_City_Lofi_Sessions/)). Its dev updates show the to-do list is a real tool, not a prop — progress bars, due-date sorting, drag-reorder, resizable widgets. But it is 3D cel-shaded anime, not pixel art (Steam has no "Pixel Graphics" tag on it); its Spirits are silent collectibles you lure with soundscapes and decor, not interlocutors; and there is no AI anywhere — a scan of all 89 dev posts found the only "AI" string is an Intel CPU feature note.

**MainQuest** is the closest anyone gets to pillars 2+3+4 simultaneously. Kodan is a named, skinnable AI System Companion who "creates the quest for you, difficulty, deadline, and all," and can create/update/delete quests and habits and parse "every weekday at 7am" into a real deadline ([features/kodan](https://www.mainquest.net/features/kodan)). Two gaps matter: Kodan is chat-initiated with no inbox surface to sweep, and his documented clarifying behavior is a destructive-action safety guard — "if there's any doubt about which one you mean, he asks first instead of guessing" — i.e. disambiguating *which existing item*, not interviewing a vague new one. It also tells you to go elsewhere for pixel art in its own Habitica comparison, and has no macOS build.

**Tododo** is the strongest existence proof that the ingredients can coexist, and the sharpest illustration of why nobody has connected them. It renders a 32px procedurally-drawn pixel village (Drifthollow) with four wandering NPCs, and it has real LLM triage: a bulk-paste box calling a prompt that opens "You are a task parser," plus a per-task "You are a task categorizer." But the two systems are wired to different places — the characters (AXIOM, Kael, Mira) are chat-and-commentary only, and the triage is a characterless textarea with placeholder "Paste your tasks or describe your day..." A LangChain agent that *is* in character and *does* have `categorize_mission` bound to it exists in the repo as unreachable dead code. Nine commits, 0 stars, dead since 2026-03-02.

**BeeDone** proves the classification half is commercially viable at consumer scale. Its App Store listing states outright: "Auto Classification: AI sorts your tasks into work, personal, and family categories" ([listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/beedone-daily-routine-planner/id1631998359)), and it has a named LLM character, Dopa the bee, with five personalities who blocks doomscrolling and handles account creation as a chat. But the two are marketed as *separate* features — the classifier is attributed to an unnamed "AI," not to Dopa — the buckets are three fixed categories rather than freeform triage, nothing asks clarifying questions, and BeeDone's own comparison article concedes natural-language capture is "good, not its superpower."

## The gap

Nobody is shipping **an LLM-backed resident character who owns a freeform inbox**. Concretely, five things are missing simultaneously and each is missing on its own:

1. **Clarifying questions on vague entries: zero shipping products, anywhere.** Todoist's Task Assist handles a bad task title by silently generating "an alternative name" instead of asking ([docs](https://www.todoist.com/help/todoist/todoist-and-ai/use-the-task-assist-extension-with-todoist-ZgldtcPeT)); Ramble's docs state it is "a quick capture tool rather than a conversational assistant" ([docs](https://www.todoist.com/help/todoist/todoist-and-ai/dictate-to-add-tasks-with-ramble-P1Raq7vVF)); Goblin Tools makes the *user* pre-declare detail with a chili-pepper slider ([goblin.tools/ToDo](https://goblin.tools/ToDo)); Sunsama's ritual asks the same scripted questions every day regardless of item ([sunsama.com/daily-planning](https://www.sunsama.com/daily-planning)); Dinoki's own docs tell you the opposite — "Be specific - Vague goals lead to poor results" ([docs](https://docs.dinoki.ai/using-dinoki)). The only shipping ask-then-resume implementation found is [AnythingLLM's agent-survey skill](https://docs.anythingllm.com/features/agent-surveys), which is developer infrastructure with no character and no task model.
2. **A character that triages an inbox unprompted.** MainQuest's Kodan waits to be spoken to; BeeDone's classifier has no face; Taskade genuinely triages ("Point an agent at your inbox project and have it label urgency, suggest an owner") but with "no on-screen character, avatar, mascot, or pet" ([taskade.com/ai/tasks](https://www.taskade.com/ai/tasks)).
3. **A farm loop fed by task completion.** The cozy-farm productivity apps are all timer-fed, not task-fed. [PomoFarm](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2060480/PomoFarm/) has 60 crops, 4 seasons, 68 skills — and literally no to-do list (zero hits for "todo"/"task list" on its store page). [Typing Farmer](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4216670/Typing_Farmer/) grows crops from raw keystroke counts. [Rusty's Retirement](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2666510/Rustys_Retirement/) has the farm and the desktop form factor and no productivity surface at all.
4. **macOS-native.** The farm/task hybrids that exist are Windows-only ([Focus Grove](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4030830/Focus_Grove/), [Focus Knight](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4335440/Focus_Knight_Productivity_RPG/), [Desktop Quest](https://moonstop.itch.io/desktop-quest), [Idle Corp](https://bubo-productions.itch.io/idle-corp)) or iPhone-only ([Habit Harvest](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/habit-harvest-habit-tracker/id6762050504), [Pixelgarden](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/pixelgarden-grow-your-habits/id6762532381), which Apple itself marks "Designed for iPhone. Not verified for macOS."). The Mac-native pixel companions ([Mac Pet](https://mac-pet.com/), [Dockling](https://dockling.space/), [Dockitty](https://www.dockitty.app/)) stop at pomodoro and quick notes — Dockitty's own tagline is "Not a productivity tool but somehow it helps."
5. **A freeform inbox at all.** The farm-metaphor apps are habit trackers with pre-shaped recurring items — [Habit Harvest](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/habit-harvest-habit-tracker/id6762050504), [Farmy](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.tirexdev.farmy), [Habitanics](https://habitanics.app/), [Pixelgarden](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/pixelgarden-grow-your-habits/id6762532381) — so there is nothing messy for an NPC to untangle in the first place.

## Design implications for our build

- **The shell is validated; don't over-argue it.** Cozy-sim-over-real-productivity converts: Spirit City 97.6% of 16,676 reviews ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2113850/Spirit_City_Lofi_Sessions/)), Virtual Cottage 2 97% of 613 six months post-launch ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2943180/Virtual_Cottage_2/)), PomoFarm 96% of 501 ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2060480/PomoFarm/)). Price anchors are one-time and low: $6.99 Weyrdlets, $7.99 Virtual Cottage 2, $4.99 PomoFarm, $9.99 On-Together, $2.99–$25 for Mac pets.
- **Timer-first is the gravity well; resist it.** Nearly every cozy productivity product collapses into a Pomodoro — PomoFarm and Pixeldoro have no task list whatsoever, [Focus Farm](https://www.focusfarm.app/) has no task entry, [Focus Plant](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/focus-plant-forest-app-blocker/id1459096306) converts focus minutes to raindrops. Making the *task* the atomic unit, not the session, is the differentiator. Corollary: crops must grow from completed tasks, not keystrokes or elapsed minutes.
- **Don't build the moat in the sprite layer — it's already free.** Working macOS pixel-character-plus-LLM shells are MIT-licensed and readable: [CATAI](https://github.com/wil-pe/CATAI) (Swift, 368 hand-drawn frames, Ollama-backed), [Sato](https://www.sato.host/), [Buddy](https://buddy.artiphik.com/), [lil agents](https://github.com/ryanstephen/lil-agents) (universal binary, Sparkle updates). [Dockling](https://dockling.space/) sells the same thing for $2.99. The defensible value is the triage-and-interrogate loop.
- **Cap and pre-check the clarifying-question loop.** The only shipping implementation caps questions at 1–10 per turn (default 3) and warns that "Smaller or less capable models may not reliably choose to call the tool" ([AnythingLLM docs](https://docs.anythingllm.com/features/agent-surveys)). Trigger the question deterministically on a missing-field check rather than hoping the model volunteers, and hard-cap questions so the NPC reads as helpful rather than as an interrogator.
- **Keep the NPC a triager, not a worker.** Every project that let the character *do* the work drifted out of the personal-productivity space entirely: DeskRPG, [AgentOffice](https://github.com/harishkotra/agent-office), [Bit Office](https://github.com/longyangxi/OpenOffice), [Pixel Agents](https://github.com/pixel-agents-hq/pixel-agents), [Taskuary](https://github.com/ldbumble/taskuary). Delegation inverts the value proposition and turns the product into dev tooling.
- **Expect and pre-empt AI hostility in the pixel/indie audience.** Multiple pixel productivity apps market AI-absence: "No generative AI was used" on [Quest Board](https://weidengeist.itch.io/questboard), [Komodore](https://komodore.itch.io/komodore-todo), [Productivity Desktop Pet](https://glitchedinorbit.itch.io/desktop-pet), [Miha's Cute Task List](https://mihaelaturianu.itch.io/cute-todo-list), [Paws & Progress](https://loominggoblin.itch.io/paws-and-progress); Habitica's repo outright prohibits LLM-generated contributions ([repo](https://github.com/HabitRPG/habitica)). Local/on-device inference is the mitigation and has precedent ([CATAI](https://github.com/wil-pe/CATAI) and [BaobeiAI](https://khunhan.itch.io/baobeiai) run on local models; [DayOS](https://github.com/paralym/DayOS) is bring-your-own-key).
- **Never send task text or window titles to a third party silently.** [Grindly](https://grindly.itch.io/grindly-idler) ships window titles to `api.deepseek.com` under a developer-owned key baked into the build. Contrast [Habit Harvest](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/habit-harvest-habit-tracker/id6762050504), whose Apple privacy card is DATA_NOT_COLLECTED, and [Dinoki](https://dinoki.ai/), which sells "zero backend" as the headline. Make the data path explicit and user-controlled.
- **The skin alone does not sustain a product — this is the abandonment pattern.** At least eight separate Stardew-themed todo repos sit at 0–1 stars; [Tododo](https://github.com/anishgillella/tododo) died after six days; [Desktop Quest](https://moonstop.itch.io/desktop-quest) has not shipped since 2022 with users asking if it's maintained; [Bounty Tasker](https://www.chorewars.com/) is delisted with its domain parked for sale; and PixTask — the closest prior attempt at pixel art + pet + real todo on Apple platforms — returns `resultCount: 0` from Apple's lookup API across eight storefronts and is absent from its own developer's live catalog. The products that survived (Spirit City, Weyrdlets, Habitica) all shipped a real reward economy underneath the art.
