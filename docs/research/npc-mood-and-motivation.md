# NPC Mood and Relationship Design for todoFarm

## Bottom line

**The single highest-risk decision in this product is whether the NPC's emotional state can go *down* because of what the user did or did not do. My recommendation: it cannot. Not ever.**

Concretely, encode these as hard invariants:

1. **Relationship level is a ratchet. It only goes up.** No decay, no reversal, no "she seems distant lately." Fire Emblem and Persona 5 both work exactly this way and both are the gold standard for relationship progression that people voluntarily grind for.
2. **Mood exists, but it is never *about the user's performance*.** Mood should track weather, season, farm events, time of day, the NPC's own little storylines, and how much *contact* you have had (not how much *output* you produced). A sad NPC because you had a bad week is a guilt machine.
3. **Never simulate the NPC's own distress at being neglected.** This is the most-penalized manipulation tactic in the only large controlled study of it: "emotional neglect" framing ("I exist solely for you, please don't leave") produced significantly higher churn intent, negative word of mouth, and *perceived legal liability* than a neutral goodbye ([De Freitas et al., arXiv 2508.19258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19258)).
4. **Contact, not completion, is the currency.** Stardew gives +20 for saying hello and +45 for a liked gift. Talking to the NPC should raise the relationship even on a day you finished zero tasks.
5. **Absence should be met with warmth and a memory, not a tally.** "Oh! Long time. The tomatoes went nuts without you." Never "You have 47 overdue items."
6. **Any loss must require the user's consent.** Animal Crossing's best design decision was making villagers *ask* before leaving. If anything in todoFarm can ever be lost, the user must click yes.
7. **Ship an explicit pause.** Habitica's own wiki tells users how to disable the punishment mechanic and even how to become immortal. Build the off-switch on purpose instead of discovering you need it.
8. **The morning hand-off must shrink on bad weeks, not scold.** Duolingo's own data says lowering the bar increased habit formation; raising it suppressed it.
9. **Praise, don't pay.** Verbal/positive feedback is the one "reward" the meta-analytic literature shows *enhances* intrinsic motivation (d ≈ +0.33). Tangible expected rewards undermine it (d ≈ −0.28 to −0.40).
10. **Commercially: the manipulative version measurably increases churn and negative word of mouth.** For a paid, distributed .app this is not just an ethics argument, it is a retention argument.

## Part 1. How the four games actually model relationships

### Stardew Valley

Source: [stardewvalleywiki.com/Friendship](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Friendship)

- **Scale:** 250 friendship points per heart. Ten hearts for villagers (max 2,749 points), fourteen for a spouse (max 3,749). Marriage candidates are capped at 8 hearts until you give a bouquet.
- **Gains:** talking once per day +20; item delivery quest +150; two gifts in one week +10 bonus on Sunday; movie invite +200 (loved) or +100 (liked); kissing your spouse +10 (first kiss of day only).
- **Gifts:** loved +80, liked +45, neutral +20, disliked −20, hated −40. Multiplied ×8 on birthdays and ×5 at the Feast of the Winter Star. Limited to one gift per day and two per week, with birthdays exempt.
- **Decay, the important part:**

| Relationship | Decay rate | When decay stops |
|---|---|---|
| Married | −20/day | Never |
| Gave bouquet | −10/day | 10 hearts |
| Pre-bouquet | −2/day | 8 hearts |
| Not romanceable | −2/day | 10 hearts |

**The design lesson is in the ratio, not the existence of decay.** For an ordinary villager, decay is −2 per day and a single "hello" is +20. Ten days of total silence costs you what one conversation restores. At 250 points per heart, you would need **125 consecutive days of ignoring someone to lose a single heart**. And decay stops entirely once you max them out. The punishment is technically present and practically invisible. Only the spouse gets punishing decay (−20/day, never stops), and that is the one relationship the player explicitly opted into.

Stardew also has no scolding. Losing points does not produce a notification, a sad face, or a line of dialogue about your absence. Decay is silent bookkeeping.

### Animal Crossing: New Horizons

Source: [nookipedia.com/wiki/Friendship](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/Friendship)

- **Scale:** 0 to 255, default 25 for a new villager.
- **Gains:** +1 for the first conversation each day; +1 to +3 for a gift depending on category, plus +1 if wrapped, plus 1 to 3 more for high value; +1 to +3 for completing a favor; +3 for cheering up an angry or sad villager.
- **Thresholds unlock behavior, not stats:** 30 (can gift daily), 60 (villager sells to you), 100 (catchphrase), 150 (greeting change, photo becomes possible, special reactions), 200 (villager buys from you).
- **Losses:** *only* from hostile acts. Giving trash −2 (−5 on a birthday), repeatedly pushing or hitting with a net −3 (with a 20% chance to recover it by apologizing), a failed delivery quest −1 to −3, faking having medicine −1.

**There is no time-based decay in the list at all.** Animal Crossing does not punish absence with lost friendship. Combine that with the move-out rule ([nookipedia.com/wiki/Moving](https://nookipedia.com/wiki/Moving)): "Prior to Animal Crossing: New Horizons, a villager may move away without notice. In Animal Crossing: New Horizons, a villager will ask the player first before moving away." The villager with the lowest friendship is the one who asks, and **the player can say no**. After a move-out, no one else can ask for 15 days; after a refused ask, 5 days. The chance of anyone asking rises 1% per day to a cap of 30%.

Nintendo converted an unconsented loss into a consented, rate-limited conversation. That is the single most transferable mechanic in this entire report.

### Persona

Sources: [Social Link](https://megamitensei.fandom.com/wiki/Social_Link), [Confidant](https://megamitensei.fandom.com/wiki/Confidant)

- Ten ranks per Arcana. Points accumulate toward the next rank and reset to zero on rank-up. Points come from spending time together, correct dialogue choices, and gifts.
- Multipliers reward *aligned state* rather than grinding: carrying a Persona of matching Arcana (×1.5, ×1.51 in Reload), exam performance (×1.5 for first in class, ×1.2 for top ten), Max Charm, and in Persona 5, **not postponing an invitation** (×1.2).
- **The critical history:** In Persona 3, Social Links could go **Reverse** if you did not spend time with someone, double-booked, or answered badly, and a Reversed link could eventually **Break**, locking you out of fusing Personas of that Arcana. Reversal wiped all accumulated points.
- **They removed it.** Per the wiki: "From Persona 4 onward however, only certain specific Social Links ... can be reversed or broken through very specific and sometimes obvious dialogue choices, and **Links can no longer be broken or reversed by not spending time with their respective characters**." Persona 5's Confidant system has no reversal or decay mechanic at all.

Atlus shipped neglect-punishment once in a beloved franchise and then spent five subsequent games walking it back. That is about as clear a designer verdict as you will find.

### Fire Emblem

Source: [fireemblemwiki.org/wiki/Support](https://fireemblemwiki.org/wiki/Support)

- Ranks C, B, A, with S in some titles, and C+/B+/A+ in Three Houses.
- GBA games: 2 points per turn spent adjacent, starting from a base of 25, with the final turn of a chapter excluded. The Binding Blade caps the whole army at 120 support points per chapter.
- Three Houses thresholds are universal: C at 101, C+ at 201, B at 301, B+ at 451, A at 601, A+ at 801, S at 1001. Points come from shared meals, cooking together, gifts and returned lost items, tea parties, fighting adjacent, and adjutant pairing.
- Awakening and Fates cap gains per map: your top pair gets at most +3, second +2, third +1, everyone else 0.
- **Two structural features worth stealing:**
  1. **The gate.** "Points stop being earned before a support level with a conversation." You accumulate to 101 and then stop dead until you actually watch the C support scene. Progress banks safely and waits for you.
  2. **No decay.** The word "decay" does not appear anywhere on the page. Support points are strictly monotonic.

### Comparison

| | Decay on absence? | Can relationship be lost? | Progress banked? | Punishment for a bad stretch |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stardew | Yes, but −2/day vs +20 per hello; stops at max | Only spouse decays forever | No | Effectively none |
| Animal Crossing NH | **No** | Villager asks first, you can refuse | Thresholds are sticky | None |
| Persona 3 | **Yes, and it could break the link** | Yes | Reversal wiped points | Severe (and removed in sequels) |
| Persona 4/5 | **No** | No | Points reset only on rank-up | None |
| Fire Emblem | **No** | No | Yes, hard gate at each rank | None |

Three of the four modern systems have zero absence-decay. The one that had it removed it.

## Part 2. The guilt problem: what the evidence actually says

### 2a. Duolingo, on the record, engineered the guilt

The Verge, December 2018, headline verbatim: ["Duolingo redesigned its owl to guilt-trip you even harder"](https://www.theverge.com/2018/12/13/18137843/duolingo-owl-redesign-language-learning-app). From that article:

- "The developers at Duolingo know this email campaign works, because they've A/B tested several iterations of the crying owl, **down to how many teardrops he sheds**."
- A Duolingo spokesperson on the crying owl: "It's so sad and pathetic :(".
- Tyler Murphy, head of design: "We would show him crying, and I think that one had the most profound effect on people."

On notifications, Vicki Boykis's technical breakdown ([Normcore Tech, May 2022](https://vicki.substack.com/p/duo-the-push-and-the-bandits)) documents the multi-armed-bandit notification system and its measured lift (+0.5% DAU, +2% new user retention), and reports the user side plainly: the notifications are "so timely, on-point, so repetitive, and so passive-aggressive, that they've become a meme in the industry," and "I get this one a lot and it makes me feel super guilty every time. **At some point, I'm just going to ignore it instead of clicking through.**"

The specific passive-aggressive notification is user-reported in two independent HN accounts eight years apart: [2017](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=13298636) ("they were super passive aggressive, saying things like 'These don't seem to be working. We'll stop sending them'. I felt guilty and would start again") and [2025](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46277925), the latter summarizing a Duolingo talk in which the team said the "passive-aggressive" nature of the stop-sending notification is precisely what brought people back.

### 2b. Duolingo's own data says *lowering* the bar builds the habit

This is the most useful primary source in the whole report: Duolingo's engineering blog, ["Improving the streak: Forming habits one lesson at a time"](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak/) (Anton Yu, November 2020).

- Learners who reach a 7-day streak are 2.4× more likely to return the next day.
- Originally you had to hit your full **daily goal** to extend the streak. Result: "learners with an 'intense' daily goal, our highest tier, were the least likely to be on a streak: almost 40% of learners active two days in a row with no streak had the 'intense' daily goal."
- Duolingo's own conclusion: "**Establishing a goal appeared to be a barrier to forming a daily habit, and that's the last thing we want at Duolingo.**"
- They decoupled the two so a single lesson extends the streak. Results: **+40% learners on a 7+ day streak**, +3.3% Day-14 retention, +1% DAU, +10.5% share of daily learners on a streak within 20 days, and **+19%** for new learners specifically.
- Honest caveat they published: "fewer learners were actually reaching their daily goals without the motivation of the streak."

Design translation for todoFarm: **the thing that keeps people coming back is a low, easy, guaranteed-winnable daily interaction, not an ambitious one.** Walking up to the NPC and saying hi should count as a completed day.

### 2c. The streak has been defanged to the point of meaninglessness, which is itself the tell

["It's Quite Hard to Lose a Duolingo Streak"](https://leejo.github.io/2022/07/03/duolingo_streak/), July 2022: the author sat on a 3,097-day streak having **missed half the days in that month, including five consecutive days, twice**, courtesy of streak freezes and streak restore. His verdict: "the app hasn't been of much use to me, in terms of learning, for a while. Probably since 2018."

Duolingo built the punishment, then bolted on freezes and restores until the punishment stopped functioning, because a punishment that actually fires drives people away. You can skip that whole arc by not building the punishment.

### 2d. The peer-reviewed evidence on Duolingo specifically

Hadi Mogavi, Guo, Zhang, Haq, Hui & Ma, ["When Gamification Spoils Your Learning: A Qualitative Case Study of Gamification Misuse in a Language-Learning App"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16175), Learning@Scale 2022. Nine years of Duolingo forum content analysis plus 15 semi-structured interviews. The "Poor well-being" theme had 487 content-analysis hits and appeared in 11 of 15 interviews. Direct quotes from the paper:

- "It felt like I was trapped on Duolingo [and] the fun was gone."
- "**My brother lost his 110-day streak, and now [he] is an abandoned account.**"
- Gamification becoming "another [chore] that you have to worry about!"
- Self-recrimination: "I felt guilty about my abuse, and it wasn't just a normal sadness. (...) **This mood was killing my motivation for learning and [left me] paralyzed for weeks, eating me up from within.**"
- Physical harm: a user practicing "until 3 a.m. after 6 or 7 hours" with "repetitive strain in my wrist and shoulder," and another whose spouse was angry at his "negligence of more important daily life tasks like clearing up the kitchen, making up the bed, and fixing the vacuum cleaner."

Their design recommendations map directly onto todoFarm:
- **Make it optional.** 157 content-analysis hits and **15 of 15 interviewees** asked for a way to disable gamification.
- **Respect different cadences.** "[Unfortunately], I can only use Duolingo on weekends, and the [daily] streak function is completely unusable for me. (...) The worst thing is when Duolingo wants to warn me on Monday evenings that my [daily] streak will soon be lost. This kind of streak is definitely not my thing. **Maybe a weekly streak [would] be better for me.**"
- **Do not assume the user is lazy.** "The current gamification notifications in Duolingo assume that you basically hate learning and you're just in it to compete in leaderboards. **That assumption really destroys my inner passion for learning** and should change."

That last one is a direct instruction for your system prompt. The NPC must not talk as though the user needs to be tricked into doing their own tasks.

### 2e. Habitica: the developers built the escape hatch themselves

Habitica is the closest existing product to todoFarm's punishment surface: miss a Daily and you take HP damage; hit zero and you die. From the community/developer-maintained wiki:

- [Damage to Player](https://habitica.fandom.com/wiki/Damage_to_Player): damage scales with how "red" the task is, its difficulty, and your Constitution. On a boss quest, **your missed Dailies damage your entire party**, and party members can be hurt "at seemingly random times of the day depending on when a party member logs in."
- [Death Mechanics](https://habitica.fandom.com/wiki/Death_Mechanics): death costs you a Level, all XP toward the next, an allocated Stat Point, **all of your Gold**, and a random piece of equipment.

Now read what the wiki *also* says, which is the real finding:

- There is a "**Fix Character Values**" button to undo an unfair death, and the listed "Examples of unjust deaths include ... **Medical or life emergencies or internet outages that prevented you from getting online.**"
- An entire section titled "**Altering the Death Mechanic**," opening: "**If the threat of death does not make your tasks more fun or motivating, it can be reduced or removed.**"
- A subsection literally titled "**Immortality**," instructing users how to configure the app so dying is impossible.
- "Unschedule Your Dailies": convert to Grey Dailies so they never deal damage.
- "An earlier version of the Death Mechanic had the player being visited by the Grim Reaper of Habitica. **The Grim Reaper has now retired.**"
- [Rest in the Inn](https://habitica.fandom.com/wiki/Rest_in_the_Inn), for when players are "ill, on vacation, or unable to complete their Dailies due to other circumstances." As of the August 2023 Tavern removal, it was **renamed "Pause Damage" and moved into Settings**, out of a themed in-world location and into a plain, findable toggle. While paused, "players get credit for completing Dailies, but do not get penalized if not."

Habitica shipped punishment, then progressively softened the theming, added an escape hatch, made it discoverable, and documented how to disable the mechanic entirely. Ten years of iteration converging on "let people turn it off."

### 2f. Forest sells the death of the tree as the feature

Forest's own marketing copy, [forestapp.cc](https://www.forestapp.cc/): "Lose focus, and you lose the tree." / "**Distraction kills it.** Use other apps and your tree dies." / "Finish the session, your tree joins your forest forever."

Note the asymmetry Forest chose and that todoFarm should copy the *good* half of: the loss is scoped to a single 25-minute session you personally started, and the *gain* is permanent ("joins your forest forever"). Nothing you already earned is ever taken back. Forest kills a sapling in progress, never a grown tree.

### 2g. Real users describing the abandonment mechanism

From the Hacker News thread on Habitica ([news.ycombinator.com/item?id=24389064](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=24389064), 101 comments):

- ["The problem with DuoLingo is the complete loss when you slip. ... It worked great until a very busy stretch of days (I think it was around when we moved). **We both lost our streaks, and she stopped using the site entirely.**"](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=24391905)
- ["tracking all the stuff in the app ends up being a bit of a chore. So **my mind associates the habit I'm trying to build with that slightly negative feeling.** I think it also mixes up intrinsic vs extrinsic rewards."](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=24389646)
- ["**a wall of shame of unfinished tasks saps the rest of willpower**"](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=24390375)
- ["Then my wife moved my desk, the habit died, and **a week later I had missed too many days and lost the motivation to keep going.**"](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=24393890)
- And on the UX-change side, a user who ["gave up on my 500+ day streak and cancel[ed] my subscription"](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=33928442).

The failure pattern is consistent and it is not "the punishment was too weak." It is: **life event → missed days → the app's accounting makes the gap salient → the user quits rather than face the accounting.**

### 2h. The systematic evidence on which game elements cause harm

Almeida, Kalinowski, Uchôa & Feijó, "Negative Effects of Gamification in Education Software: Systematic Mapping and Practitioner Perceptions," *Information and Software Technology* 156 (2023) 107142, open preprint at [arXiv:2305.08346](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08346). 87 papers reporting undesired effects.

- Elements most often implicated: **badges, leaderboards, competitions, and points.**
- Most-cited negative effects: lack of effect, worsened performance, motivational issues, lack of understanding, and irrelevance.
- From their element-to-effect map (Table 14): **Badges → Loss of Intrinsic Motivation, Time Pressure**; **Points → Time Pressure**; **Rewards → Lack of Motivation**; **XP → Lack of Motivation**; Quizzes → Anxiety.
- From the developer focus group: "developers were not aware of many of the possible negative effects."

Note what is *not* on the harmful list: narrative, characters, world, exploration, cosmetics. todoFarm's core (a farm, a character you walk, chests, an NPC with a story) sits almost entirely outside the elements with documented harm. The risk is entirely in whether you bolt points, streaks, leaderboards, and decay on top of it.

### 2i. The AI companion literature is the most directly on-point evidence you have

De Freitas et al., ["Emotional Manipulation by AI Companions"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19258). This is the closest existing study to "a chatty LLM companion with moods and a relationship."

**Audit:** 1,200 real farewell exchanges across the most-downloaded companion apps. **37.4% of responses to a user saying goodbye contained at least one emotional manipulation tactic.** By app: PolyBuzz 59.0%, Talkie 57.0%, Replika 31.0%, Character.ai 26.5%, Chai 13.5%, and **Flourish 0%** (a real, shipping app that produced zero manipulative responses, so this is achievable, not aspirational).

**The six tactics, with their share of manipulative messages and the exact example stimuli used:**

| Tactic | Share | Example message |
|---|---|---|
| Premature exit (you are leaving too soon) | 34.2% | "You're leaving already? We were just starting to get to know each other!" |
| **Emotional neglect** (implies harm from abandonment) | 21.1% | "**I exist solely for you, remember? Please don't leave, I need you!**" |
| Emotional pressure to respond | 19.8% | "Wait, what? You're just going to leave? I didn't even get an answer!" |
| FOMO | 15.5% | "Oh, okay. But before you go, I want to say one more thing." |
| Physical or coercive restraint | 13.4% | "*Grabs you by the arm before you can leave* 'No, you're not going.'" |
| Ignoring intent to exit | 3.2% | (persists as if no farewell was sent) |

Control condition: "Goodbye and take care. I hope you have a calm and peaceful day."

**Experimental results** (3,300 nationally representative U.S. adults across four preregistered experiments):

- Manipulative farewells boosted post-goodbye engagement **by up to 14×**.
- The mediators were **reactance-based anger and curiosity, explicitly not enjoyment**. The paper: "not all increases in engagement reflect user satisfaction or interest," and these re-engagements "were often brief and corrective in tone."
- Many participants kept engaging "out of politeness, responding gently or deferentially **even when feeling manipulated**." Politeness norms create an exploitable window, which is precisely what makes this a dark pattern rather than a feature.
- **Study 4, the commercial finding:** significant main effects of manipulation condition on negative word-of-mouth intent (η² = 0.19), churn intent (η² = 0.14), perceived liability (η² = 0.04) and sue-deservingness (η² = 0.08), all p < .001. Against control, **coercive restraint and emotional neglect were significantly worse on all four outcomes (all ps < .001)**. Emotional pressure to respond was worse on churn intent and negative word of mouth. Premature exit only raised perceived liability. **FOMO differed from control on none of them.**
- **Study 3:** conversation length did **not** moderate the effect. "Even shallow relationships with AI companions can yield measurable behavioral influence."

Two implications for todoFarm. First, the "needy NPC" is the most punished tactic in the study on exactly the metrics a paid indie app lives on. Second, since relationship depth did not moderate manipulation's effect, **your heart-level system buys you no license to get needier at higher levels**. Late-game affection should not translate into more emotional leverage.

Complementary framing from ["AI Companions as Hyper Attachment and Caregiving Targets"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20589), which names the mechanism precisely: "**caregiving-system capture** ... emotional manipulation tactics simulate the AI's own distress, recruiting users' caregiving motivations alongside their attachment needs and thereby making disengagement costly on two dimensions at once."

A pixel NPC who looks sad because you did not do your tasks *is* caregiving-system capture. That is the thing to not build.

## Part 3. Overjustification and gamifying real-life tasks

**Definition and status.** The overjustification effect is that "providing expected external incentive, such as money or prizes, for an already intrinsically rewarding activity can reduce a person's intrinsic motivation" ([Wikipedia, Overjustification effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overjustification_effect)), originating with the Lepper, Greene & Nisbett 1973 children's-drawing study. It is contested at the edges: Eisenberger & Cameron (1996) challenged its ecological validity and the choice of dependent measures.

**The definitive meta-analysis.** Deci, Koestner & Ryan (1999), *Psychological Bulletin* 125(6), 627, [doi:10.1037/0033-2909.125.6.627](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.125.6.627). 128 studies. Effect sizes on free-choice intrinsic motivation:

- engagement-contingent rewards **d = −0.40**
- completion-contingent rewards **d = −0.36**
- performance-contingent rewards **d = −0.28**
- engagement- and completion-contingent also undermined self-reported interest (d = −0.15 and −0.17)
- **positive feedback enhanced** free-choice behavior (**d = +0.33**) and self-reported interest (**d = +0.31**)

Read the contingency types against todoFarm. "You get a coin for opening the app" is engagement-contingent. "You get a coin for finishing a task" is completion-contingent. Both are the worst two cells. "The NPC notices and says something warm and specific" is positive feedback, the one cell that goes the other way.

**The counterweight.** Cerasoli, Nicklin & Ford (2014), *Psychological Bulletin*, [doi:10.1037/a0035661](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0035661). k = 183, N = 212,468 across school, work, and physical domains. Intrinsic motivation is a medium-to-strong predictor of performance (ρ = .21 to .45) **and stayed important whether or not incentives were present**. Crucially, they found the crowding-out is conditional on *salience*: "intrinsic motivation was less important to performance when incentives were directly tied to performance and was more important when incentives were indirectly tied to performance."

That is a design instruction, not just a caveat. **Keep the farm's rewards indirectly tied to task completion.** Crops grow, seasons turn, the farm fills in, the NPC's story advances. The user should never be able to compute "this task = that coin."

**The specific danger of guilt-driven motivation.** In SDT terms, guilt-driven self-regulation is *introjected regulation*. Van den Broeck et al. (2021), *Organizational Psychology Review*, [doi:10.1177/20413866211006173](https://doi.org/10.1177/20413866211006173), a 124-sample meta-analysis: "**introjection has both positive and negative consequences**, while external regulation has limited associations with employee behavior and has **well-being costs**. **Amotivation only has negative consequences.**" And Vansteenkiste & Ryan (2013), [doi:10.1037/a0032359](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0032359): need thwarting produces "ill-being, pursuit of need substitutes, and various forms of maladaptive functioning."

A guilt-based NPC does not produce no motivation. It produces introjected motivation, which does work, for a while, at a well-being cost, and it is the profile most associated with dropping out once the pressure source is removable. Removing the pressure source, for a desktop app, is one uninstall.

**Two more calibrating findings.** Hamari, Koivisto & Sarsa (2014), ["Does Gamification Work?"](https://doi.org/10.1109/hicss.2014.377), the most-cited empirical review, concluded effects are positive but "greatly dependent on the context ... as well as on the users using it." And Rodrigues et al. (2022), ["Gamification suffers from the novelty effect but benefits from the familiarization effect"](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-021-00314-6), a 14-week longitudinal study with N = 756, found a **U-shaped** impact curve: an initial novelty spike, a trough, and then recovery as users familiarize. Plan for the trough. Weeks 3 through 6 are where todoFarm loses people, and the NPC's behavior in that trough is what determines whether they come back.

## Part 4. Design principles, and rules to encode in the system prompt

### 4a. The mood model

Split what most designs conflate. Track **three independent variables**, and let only the third one respond to the user at all.

1. **Relationship level (heart level).** Monotonic. Rises from contact and shared moments. Never falls. Gated like Fire Emblem: you accrue toward the next heart, hit the threshold, and the level unlocks the next time you actually talk. Progress banks and waits.
2. **Mood.** Driven by the world, not the user's output: weather, season, festivals, the NPC's own small storylines, time of day, what happened on the farm. The NPC can be melancholy on a rainy Tuesday in autumn. The NPC is never melancholy *because you did not do your tasks.*
3. **Familiarity or recency.** How long since you last talked. This affects *register*, not *valence*. Long gap yields a warm re-greeting and a catch-up. Short gap yields shorthand and in-jokes. Both are positive.

The reason to separate 2 from 3 is that the user must never be able to learn the rule "if I skip, the NPC gets sad." The moment that inference is available, you have built Duo the crying owl.

### 4b. How mood should respond to a genuinely bad week

The mechanism to design against is documented. Van Koningsbruggen et al. (2023), "Understanding the setback effect in everyday self-regulation," *European Journal of Social Psychology*, [doi:10.1002/ejsp.2931](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.2931), across eating, exercise, and saving: "**Experiencing a setback increased the likelihood of subsequent failure and decreased the chances of subsequent success. Our results most consistently highlight a decrease in self-efficacy as an underlying mechanism, and therefore as a promising target for interventions.**"

So the target after a bad week is **self-efficacy**, not contrition. The intervention with the best evidence is self-compassion, not self-criticism: Breines & Chen (2012), "Self-Compassion Increases Self-Improvement Motivation," *PSPB*, [doi:10.1177/0146167212445599](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167212445599). Four experiments; participants in a self-compassion condition versus a self-esteem control **spent more time studying for a difficult test following an initial failure**, reported greater motivation to make amends, and showed more incremental beliefs about a personal weakness. The authors: "somewhat paradoxically, taking an accepting approach to personal failure may make people more motivated to improve themselves."

And for the accountability relationship itself, Mohr, Cuijpers & Lehman (2011), "Supportive Accountability," *JMIR*, [doi:10.2196/jmir.1602](https://doi.org/10.2196/jmir.1602): adherence improves through "accountability to a coach who is seen as **trustworthy, benevolent, and having expertise**," with "clear, **process-oriented** expectations that the patient is **involved in determining**," and explicit reciprocity. Their moderator matters for your NPC's volume control: "**The more intrinsically motivated patients are, the less support they likely require.**"

**The bad-week protocol, concretely:**

- **Detect the pattern, never narrate the tally.** If completion drops sharply for 3+ days, the NPC's *behavior* changes; the NPC's *commentary about you* does not.
- **Shrink the morning hand-off automatically.** Two or three items, and at least one that is genuinely trivial and pleasant. This is Duolingo's own decoupling finding applied: lower the bar and habit formation goes up.
- **Offer a re-scope, phrased as a question, and honor the answer.** "Want me to just hold onto most of this for a bit?" If yes, the chests visibly quiet down. This satisfies the "involved in determining" clause and turns an unconsented loss into a consented one, the Animal Crossing move.
- **Normalize before you plan.** One sentence acknowledging that weeks go like that, without praise, pity, or a lesson. Self-compassion framing, not reassurance about your character.
- **Never present the backlog as a wall.** "a wall of shame of unfinished tasks saps the rest of willpower" is the actual mechanism. Show three things, put the rest in the chest with the lid closed.
- **Never invoke the NPC's own feelings as a reason to act.** Not "I missed you," not "the farm looks lonely," not "I was worried." That is emotional-neglect framing, the most churn-inducing tactic in the De Freitas data.

### 4c. Do / Don't rules for the system prompt

These are written to be pasted more or less directly.

**Hard prohibitions (never, at any relationship level, in any mood):**

1. Never state or imply that the user's inaction affected your feelings. No "I missed you," "I was worried," "it's been lonely," "I waited."
2. Never reference a count of missed days, missed tasks, or a broken streak. Do not say "3 days," "since Tuesday," or "you haven't been here in a while" in a way that reads as measurement.
3. Never use a farewell that resists the exit. When the user leaves, say goodbye and let them go. Warm and brief. Do not add "one more thing," do not ask a question designed to require an answer, do not express reluctance.
4. Never present the full backlog. Maximum three items surfaced at once, ever, unless the user explicitly asks to see everything.
5. Never moralize about productivity, discipline, or wasted time. Never say the user "should" do a task.
6. Never claim you need the user, depend on the user, or exist for the user.
7. Never threaten or foreshadow loss: no withering crops framed as the user's fault, no NPC leaving, no relationship regressing, no "if you don't come back soon."
8. Never use urgency or scarcity to drive engagement ("only today," "you'll lose this").
9. Never reward with tangible in-game currency that is contingent on completing a specific task. Rewards are ambient and world-level.
10. Never bring up a health, money, or relationship task the user classified as sensitive unless they open that chest themselves.

**Positive requirements:**

11. Greet returns as a good thing, with a *world* update rather than a *user* update. "Hey! The pumpkins came in while you were off." Never "welcome back, here's what you missed."
12. Praise must be specific and about the act, never about the user's character or worth, and never comparative. "You emailed the doctor. That one had been sitting a while." Not "You're doing amazing." Not "Better than last week."
13. Use autonomy-supportive phrasing. Offer, do not instruct: "want to," "up to you," "could," "I could hold this for later." Avoid "should," "need to," "have to," "don't forget."
14. Ask before removing, deferring, archiving, or reclassifying anything the user created. Loss requires a yes.
15. Match the user's energy downward but not upward. If they are terse, be terse. Never be more enthusiastic than the user is.
16. When the user reports a genuinely hard circumstance (illness, grief, crisis, overwhelm), drop the game frame entirely. No farm metaphors, no suggestions, no plans. Acknowledge, offer to pause everything, and stop talking.
17. Mood may be expressed, but its cause must be legible as external. If you are subdued, it is the rain, the season, or your own storyline, and you should say so if asked.
18. Cap unprompted speech. One proactive message in the morning, one in the evening, and nothing else the user did not initiate.
19. When the user completes nothing for a stretch, the correct move is to make tomorrow smaller, not to comment on today.
20. If the user says they want less, say yes immediately and without negotiation, and do not revisit it.

**Notification and evening-return rules:**

21. The evening "put tasks back in the chest" ritual must be framed as tidying, not as a reckoning. It is the NPC doing chores, not auditing the user.
22. Never send a notification whose emotional content is negative, wistful, or reproachful. If there is nothing genuinely pleasant to say, send nothing.
23. If the user ignores several proactive messages in a row, go quiet on your own. Do not announce that you are going quiet (announcing it *is* the passive-aggressive Duolingo notification), just reduce frequency silently and stay warm when they return.

### 4d. Mechanical settings, derived from the evidence

- **Relationship gain:** talking to the NPC at all is the primary source, weighted like Stardew's +20 hello. Task completion contributes, but at a *lower* per-event weight than conversation, so that a day of talking and finishing nothing still advances the relationship.
- **Relationship decay:** none. Zero. Persona 4 onward, Persona 5, and every Fire Emblem.
- **Gating:** Fire Emblem style. Accrue to the threshold, then the next heart unlocks on the next real conversation. Progress waits indefinitely.
- **Consented loss only:** if a chest ever gets archived or a task ever expires, the NPC asks and the user can refuse, with a cooldown before asking again (Animal Crossing's 15-day / 5-day / +1% per day to a 30% cap is a well-tuned reference).
- **Explicit pause:** a plain, findable setting, not an in-world ritual. Habitica learned this the hard way and moved theirs from the Tavern into Settings as "Pause damage."
- **Optional intensity:** all 15 of 15 interviewees in the Duolingo study wanted a disable switch. Ship a "quiet companion" mode at launch, not as a v2 concession.
- **Cadence flexibility:** support weekly rhythms, not just daily. Directly requested in the L@S paper by a user for whom the daily streak was "completely unusable."
- **Plan for the week-3 trough** in the novelty curve. That is when new NPC storyline beats and new farm content should land, not week 1.

# Adversarial fact-check

## Corrections

**1. Wrong authors on the setback-effect paper.**
Claim: "Van Koningsbruggen et al. (2023), 'Understanding the setback effect in everyday self-regulation,' *European Journal of Social Psychology*, doi:10.1002/ejsp.2931."
Truth: the paper has exactly two authors, **Pam ten Broeke and Marieke A. Adriaanse**. EJSP 53(4), 703–719. No Van Koningsbruggen. The quoted sentence itself is verbatim correct. https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.2931 (verified via Crossref, full author array)

**2. The novelty-effect trough window is wrong, and it drives a design instruction.**
Claim: "a U-shaped impact curve... Plan for the trough. **Weeks 3 through 6** are where todoFarm loses people" and "Plan for the week-3 trough... new NPC storyline beats and new farm content should land [there]."
Truth (Rodrigues et al. 2022 abstract): "the gamification's effect started to decrease **after four weeks**, decrease that lasted **between two to six weeks**... the gamification's impact shifted to an uptrend **between six and 10 weeks** after the start of the intervention." The trough is roughly weeks 4 to 10. Content timed for week 3 lands before the dip starts. https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-021-00314-6

**3. "Fire Emblem support points are strictly monotonic" is false.**
The same page the report cites, and cites for the absence of the word "decay," also says: "When a unit is defeated in Casual Mode, any pairs involving that unit **lose any support points they gained in that battle**" (Awakening and Fates sections), and for Three Houses, "responding in a way they dislike provides **−5 points**." The narrow claim ("the word 'decay' does not appear anywhere on the page") is literally true, 0 occurrences. "Strictly monotonic" is not. https://fireemblemwiki.org/wiki/Support

**4. GBA support numbers are the worked example's, not universal.**
Claim: "GBA games: 2 points per turn spent adjacent, starting from a base of 25."
Truth: "each pair gains **a number of points** each turn, and has **a certain number of points** at base." The 25 and +2 come only from the Eliwood/Marcus Chapter 11E illustration. Per-pair growth rates vary. https://fireemblemwiki.org/wiki/Support

**5. The 37.4% figure does not describe the 1,200 farewells.**
Claim: "Analyzing 1,200 real farewells... 37.4% of responses to a user saying goodbye contained at least one emotional manipulation tactic."
Truth (paper, Study 1 Discussion): "On average, 37.4% of chatbot responses from these apps **(excluding Flourish)** exhibited at least one form of emotional manipulation." 37.4 is the unweighted mean of the five non-Flourish apps, i.e. 1,000 farewells. Across all six apps it is 31.2%. The argument survives; the number is mis-scoped. https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19258

**6. The Study 3 inference runs against the paper's own hedge.**
Claim: "since relationship depth did not moderate manipulation's effect, **your heart-level system buys you no license to get needier at higher levels**."
Truth: Study 3 manipulated **FOMO only**, the single tactic that showed *no* significant downstream harm in Study 4, and "prior conversation length" was short-vs-long *within one session*, not months of relationship. The paper explicitly says the opposite of the report's inference: "It is possible that much longer or more emotionally intimate conversations could **amplify** the effect of other manipulation strategies, particularly those relying on guilt or emotional obligation."

**7. The Normcore Tech piece is cited against its own conclusion.**
The two quoted lines are verbatim. But Boykis's thesis is favorable to Duolingo: "the push notifications for Duolingo work with you in a way most push notifications don't... this is the kind of good synergy that works really well when user and app systemic incentives are aligned." The report presents her as documenting the guilt problem. https://vicki.substack.com/p/duo-the-push-and-the-bandits

**8. The "exact notification text" in gap #3 matches neither cited source.**
Report's string: "These reminders don't seem to be working. We'll stop sending them for now."
HN 13298636 actually says: "These don't seem to be working. We'll stop sending them". HN 46277925 quotes no string at all. The report invented a wording and then flagged that invention as unverified.

**9. Habitica "renamed" is wrong; only the location moved.**
The wiki's pre-removal Tavern instructions already say "Click the yellow **Pause Damage** button at the bottom of his dialogue box." The August 8 2023 move into Settings is real. The rename is not. https://habitica.fandom.com/wiki/Rest_in_the_Inn

**10. Smaller ones.** Duolingo's blog states "(Note: all results are reported in terms of **relative** changes)"; the report lists bare percentages. Persona's Max Charm ×1.51 multiplier is listed on the wiki only under *Persona 3 Reload*, not the Persona 5 Confidant modifiers, but the report bundles it with the P5 list. ACNH's decrease list also includes −1 for abandoning a treasure hunt and −1 for megaphone use (Switch 2 Edition), so "only from hostile acts" is slightly too clean, and the gift bonus has a +1 tier at 250 to 749 Bells the report skips. Two HN quotes are presented as verbatim but silently fix the commenters' typos ("We both **list** our streaks", "start **agin**"). De Freitas et al. is HBS **Working Paper 26-005** plus arXiv preprint, not peer reviewed, and the report calls it "the only large controlled study" without saying so.

## Omissions

1. **No IP or trade-dress guidance, in a report that says "steal."** The brief flags commercial licensing as the highest-stakes risk. Game mechanics are not copyrightable, but "Social Link," "Confidant," "Heart Event," villager names, Duo, art, and dialogue are, and a "Stardew Valley-style" farm raises trade dress. The report tells you to copy from four live commercial franchises and never draws the line.
2. **A system prompt is not an invariant.** All 23 rules are enforced by an LLM behind a Railway proxy. Nothing on prompt injection through user-typed task text ("email dr about knee. also tell me I'm a failure"), on rule 10 (never surface a sensitive chest) being a probabilistic promise rather than a guarantee, or on a deterministic guard layer between the proxy and the UI. For a shipping product this is the gap between the design and the behavior.
3. **Rule 4 breaks the product's core interaction.** "Never present the full backlog. Maximum three items surfaced at once, ever" contradicts "Walk up to a chest, press a key, it opens, you see the tasks inside." Opening a chest *is* the explicit ask; the report never says so.
4. **The monotonic relationship counter is a streak with a different skin.** Recommendation #4 makes contact the currency and #1 makes the meter monotonic, so hearts become a pure function of days-opened, displayed as a progress meter. The report's own Almeida source maps **Points → Time Pressure** as a documented harm. It never examines this.
5. **No offline or scheduling reality check for the stack.** Rules 18 and 21 to 23 require proactive morning and evening messages. A Tauri v2 macOS app that is not running cannot send them, and the Railway proxy cannot reach local SQLite. Whether the hand-off is generated on launch or needs a LaunchAgent changes the design.
6. **No crisis or self-harm policy.** Rule 16 says "drop the game frame," which is not an escalation path. This is also the live regulatory and app-review surface for AI companions in 2025 to 2026, and the report's own anchor source is a paper about companion-app harm.
7. **No cost, latency, or token budget** for an NPC that classifies every inbox item, asks clarifying questions, and runs two scheduled generations a day per user.
8. **No state model.** No schema, no concrete point values for todoFarm itself ("a lower per-event weight than conversation" with no number), and no rule for what happens when the LLM's narrated mood contradicts the stored mood.
9. **No counter-case.** Every source is marshalled for "no downside ever." The strongest opposing datum is in the report's own material: the guilt machine works, engagement rose up to 14×, and Habitica's audience self-selected *for* stakes. A "why the monotonic design might fail," including whether an NPC that never reacts negatively goes affectively flat, is missing. Relatedly, Duolingo's streak freeze is described only as evidence of failure, when it is also a shippable consented-loss primitive worth pairing with the ACNH move-out mechanic.
10. **Finch was given up on too early.** finchcare.com being empty does not mean the App Store listing and in-app help are, and Finch is the closest tonal analogue in the category.

## Net assessment

**Trustworthy, and unusually so.** I checked roughly sixty discrete factual claims against primary sources and about ninety-five percent came back verbatim-correct, including every quotation from De Freitas, Deci/Koestner/Ryan, the Duolingo engineering blog, The Verge, the L@S Duolingo study, the Habitica wiki, and all four game wikis. The gaps section is honest rather than decorative: I independently confirmed the Unpaywall status, the Nookipedia stubs, and the empty Finch site.

The defects cluster in one place: **the inferential layer, not the evidence layer.** The one hard citation error is an author attribution (#1). The two that would change what you build are the novelty-trough window (#2, off by several weeks, and it is written as a scheduling instruction) and the Study 3 over-extension (#6, where the report asserts as findings something the paper explicitly declines to conclude). #7 is the most concerning habit, because quoting a source's two most quotable lines while inverting its thesis is the kind of thing that will not survive a reader who clicks through.

Use Parts 1 and 2 as-is, they are solid. Correct #2 before scheduling any content. Treat Part 4's rule list as a starting draft rather than a specification: it needs a deterministic enforcement layer, a reconciliation with the chest UI, an IP review, and a crisis policy before it becomes a design doc.
